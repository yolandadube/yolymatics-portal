The Man

Who Was
Credo Mutwa?

Vusamazulu Credo Mutwa (1921 – 2020) was a South African Zulu Sangoma — a traditional healer, prophet, and keeper of oral history. His name means "Awakener of the Zulus".

He was recognised across Africa and internationally as a custodian of ancient African knowledge — stories, rituals, star lore, healing arts, and prophecies passed down over thousands of years.

He lived to the age of 98, spending his entire life preserving traditions that might otherwise have been lost forever.

Life Journey

Key Moments
in His Life

1921

Born in KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa, into a mixed Zulu-Catholic family.

1940s

Called to become a Sangoma after a series of visions and near-death experiences.

1964

Published Indaba, My Children — his landmark book of African oral history & legend.

1993

Recorded his famous prophecy about great migration, conflict, and the future of South Africa.

2020

Passed away at age 98, leaving behind an unparalleled legacy of African spiritual wisdom.

Spiritual Role

What Is a
Sangoma?

🌿

Healer

Uses plants, rituals, and spiritual guidance to heal the body, mind, and community.

👁️

Prophet & Seer

Receives visions and messages from ancestors to warn or guide the living.

📖

Keeper of Knowledge

Memorises and passes on thousands of years of African history, law, and sacred stories.

Credo Mutwa was regarded as the Sanusi — the highest level of Sangoma — entrusted with the most sacred and secret knowledge of his people.

November 1993

The Famous
Prophecy

In November 1993, Credo Mutwa recorded a prophecy in which he foresaw a time of great upheaval in South Africa — a period of mass human movement, conflict between different groups of people, economic strain, and social tension.

He warned that strangers would come from far lands in great numbers, that communities would clash, and that South Africa would face tests of unity and identity that would challenge the very soul of the nation.

Many people today believe these words describe the xenophobia crisis and large-scale migration that South Africa has experienced in recent decades.

Prophecy & Reality

What People
Believe It Foretold

🌊

Mass Migration

Millions of people from Zimbabwe, Mozambique, and other African nations moved into South Africa — a scale not seen before.

⚡

Xenophobic Violence

Attacks on foreign nationals erupted in 2008, 2015, and beyond — communities turning on one another.

📉

Economic Pressure

Rising unemployment and poverty fuelled tensions between locals and newcomers, just as the prophecy hinted.

🤝

A Call for Unity

Many read Mutwa's words not as doom but as a warning — a call for Africans to find unity and common humanity.

His Beliefs

Credo Mutwa's
Core Philosophy

"Africa must know herself before she can be known by others.
Our stories are not myths — they are maps of who we are."

— Credo Mutwa

🌟

Ubuntu

"I am because we are." He believed deeply in the African philosophy of communal humanity.

🌙

Ancestor Wisdom

The ancestors speak through seers. Their warnings must be honoured — not ignored — by the living.

🔥

Preservation

African knowledge is endangered. Every elder who dies without passing on their wisdom is a library burned.

Class Activities

🌿 Reflect · Discuss · Discover
  • Think & Share: In your own words, explain what a Sangoma does. Why do you think this role was so important in traditional African communities?

Final Reflection

The Legacy Lives On

"A nation that does not know its past
has no future."

— Credo Mutwa

Credo Mutwa gave his entire life to preserving the soul of Africa. His prophecies challenge us to pay attention — to our history, our communities, and the world around us.

Whether or not every prophecy comes true, the deeper message is timeless:
Know who you are. Protect your people. Listen to your elders.
