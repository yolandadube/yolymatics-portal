THE EXPANDING
UNIVERSE

The universe has been expanding ever since the Big Bang. But is this expansion constant over time?

We might assume gravity pulls matter together, slowing the expansion — much like how a cannonball shot upward gradually slows due to Earth's gravity.

The key question astronomers ask is: will expansion go on forever, or will gravity eventually win?

The answer depends on a single critical measurement — the density of matter in the universe.

🔭 Focus Question: What observations are used to determine the fate of the universe?
Concept 01

THE BIG CRUNCH

Two possible fates await the universe. It could expand forever — stars burn out, the universe grows cold and dark. Or it could collapse inward — all matter crushed into an infinitely dense point: the Big Crunch.

The Big Crunch could potentially trigger another Big Bang — leading to a cyclic pattern of expansion and contraction: a universe that breathes in and out across eternity.

The Big Bang theory tells us all hydrogen was created at the universe's inception — making these two fates the only options.

🌌
INFINITE EXPANSION
Universe expands forever. Stars exhaust their fuel. Everything fades to cold darkness — the "Heat Death" of the universe.
⚖️
CRITICAL BALANCE
Density is exactly right. Expansion slows to zero after infinite time — a perfectly poised universe, neither collapsing nor expanding indefinitely.
💥
THE BIG CRUNCH
Gravity wins. Expansion reverses. All matter collapses into a singularity — possibly triggering a new Big Bang. A cyclic universe.

Concept 02

IT'S MATTER
THAT MATTERS

The mass and pressure of matter directly influence gravity, which in turn controls the universe's fate.

Astronomers use the Hubble Constant to measure the expansion rate and overall density. Most matter has very low pressure, so density is the key variable.

Dark matter outweighs visible matter significantly — both must be factored in when calculating total density.

Density is found by dividing mass by volume, giving us the measured value of 3 × 10⁻³⁰ g/cm³.

Concept 03

CRITICAL
DENSITY

The critical density is the exact amount of matter the universe would need for its expansion to eventually stop — neither collapsing nor expanding forever.

It is determined by the current rate of expansion (the Hubble Constant).

Astronomers compare the observed density to the critical density to predict the universe's future:

Observed density > Critical density — Universe recollapses → Big Crunch
Observed density = Critical density — Flat universe, expansion halts at infinity
Observed density < Critical density — Universe expands forever → open, cold universe

Shape 01 — Positive Curvature

CLOSED
UNIVERSE

A closed universe has positive curvature — space is bent like the surface of a sphere.

This occurs when the density of matter exceeds the critical density — gravity is strong enough to overcome expansion.

If you travelled in a perfectly straight line through a closed universe, you would eventually return to your starting point — just as walking in a straight line on Earth brings you around the globe!

Shape 02 — Zero Curvature

FLAT
UNIVERSE

A flat universe has zero curvature — the geometry of space is perfectly flat, like an infinite sheet of paper.

This occurs when density equals exactly the critical density — a perfect, knife-edge balance between expansion and collapse.

Parallel light beams never cross and never diverge — they maintain a constant distance forever.

Density of matter is exactly at the critical value
Zero curvature — perfectly flat geometry
Two parallel beams of light never cross
Travel in a straight line → never return to start
Expansion stops after infinite time
Shape 03 — Negative Curvature

OPEN
UNIVERSE

An open universe has negative curvature — space is shaped like a saddle or a hyperbolic surface.

This occurs when the density is below the critical density — gravity is too weak to stop expansion. The universe expands forever, growing colder and emptier.

Parallel light beams diverge — they bend away from each other as they travel through curved space.

Low density of matter
Hyperbolic space — saddle shape
Two parallel beams of light diverge
Travel in a straight line → never return to start
Universe continues to expand — weak self-gravity cannot halt it

Concept 04

THE COSMOLOGICAL
CONSTANT

Einstein believed the universe was static — not expanding or contracting. To make his equations support this, he introduced the cosmological constant (Λ) — a term representing a force that would counteract gravity and prevent galaxies from moving apart or together.

However, Hubble's 1929 observations proved galaxies were moving away from us — the universe was expanding after all.

Einstein abandoned Λ. But decades later, astronomers discovered the accelerating expansion of the universe — and Λ came back, now interpreted as dark energy.

Concept 05

DARK
ENERGY

Astronomers can account for all known matter and energy in the universe — but it only adds up to 32% of what should be there.

The remaining 68% is unaccounted for. It cannot be detected through gravity effects or light emission. Astronomers call this unknown quantity dark energy.

Dark energy is thought to be the force driving the accelerating expansion of the universe — essentially the energy of empty space itself.

It remains one of the biggest unsolved mysteries in all of science.

CONTENTS OF THE UNIVERSE
68% — Dark Energy (unknown — drives acceleration)
27% — Dark Matter (detected via gravity)
5% — Ordinary Matter (everything we can see)
❓ The Mystery
Dark energy cannot be seen, cannot be detected by its light — only inferred from its effect on the expansion rate of the universe. We know it's there, but not what it is.

SUPERNOVA 1997ff — 10 Billion Light-Years Away
In the late 1990s, astronomers studied distant supernovae expecting to see the universe's expansion slowing down due to gravity. Instead they found the opposite — expansion was speeding up! Supernova 1997ff, discovered by the Hubble Space Telescope, provided proof of dark energy's existence by showing expansion had transitioned from slow to accelerating.

Concept 06

COSMOLOGICAL
RED-SHIFT

As the universe expands, space itself stretches — and this stretches the wavelengths of light travelling through it.

Light from distant galaxies is shifted toward the red end of the spectrum (longer wavelengths) — this is the cosmological red-shift.

The more distant a galaxy, the greater its red-shift — giving astronomers a timeline of the universe's expansion encoded in light itself.

This is how the Hubble Telescope detected the change from slow to accelerating expansion — and confirmed dark energy existed.
