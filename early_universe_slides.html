Slide 01 · Olbers' Paradox
OLBERS'
PARADOX
THE PARADOX STATES:
If the universe is infinite, static, and filled with evenly spread stars — every line of sight should eventually hit a star.
Therefore, the entire night sky should be as blindingly bright as the surface of the Sun.
🌑
But in reality — the night sky is mostly dark. This contradiction is the paradox.
⚠ A COSMOLOGICAL PUZZLE · PAVED THE WAY FOR MODERN COSMOLOGY

Slide 02 · Resolving the Paradox
THE ANSWER IS
THREE-FOLD
For the night sky to be dark, at least one of these must be TRUE
📏
FINITE UNIVERSE
The universe has a limited number of stars — not infinite. There simply aren't enough light sources to fill the sky.
⏰
TOO YOUNG
The universe is only 13.7 billion years old. Light from distant regions hasn't had enough time to reach us yet.
🔭
EXPANDING SPACE
The universe is expanding, stretching light into longer wavelengths — beyond what our eyes can see.

Slide 03 · Origin Theories
THE GREAT DEBATE
♾️
STEADY STATE
THEORY
The universe is expanding but never changes. New matter constantly appears to keep density constant. The universe has no beginning and no end.
✗ DISPROVEN · NOW OBSOLETE
VS
💥
THE BIG BANG
THEORY
Universe exploded from an infinitely hot, dense point. Matter expanded outward, density decreased. Remnant radiation still exists today.
✓ ACCEPTED BY SCIENCE TODAY
⚡ MISCONCEPTION: The Big Bang was NOT an explosion from a single point — the entire universe expanded simultaneously, with no edge.

Slide 08 · Key Terms
KNOW THESE
TERMS
Define each in your guided notes — fold to test yourself!
OLBERS' PARADOX
The contradiction between a dark night sky and the idea that an infinite universe should appear uniformly bright.
STEADY STATE THEORY
A now-obsolete theory proposing the universe expands but remains unchanged in density as new matter is created.
COSMIC HORIZON
The boundary beyond which light has not had enough time to reach us since the Big Bang.
ANTIMATTER
A mirror-opposite of ordinary matter; when matter and antimatter meet, both are annihilated into energy.
ERA OF RECOMBINATION
The period ~380,000 yrs after the Big Bang when protons and electrons first combined to form hydrogen atoms.
PRIMORDIAL NUCLEOSYNTHESIS
The production of elements heavier than hydrogen and helium in stellar cores and supernova explosions.
